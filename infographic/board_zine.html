1619–1865 · CHATTEL SLAVERY
02/ 10

SLAVERY & THE RACIAL ORDER

Built to extract labor from Black bodies — and deny their humanity.

Enslaved people were deemed property, not persons: a fabricated rationale that kept the economic engine running. Counterpoint: only a tiny share of Africans in the Atlantic trade landed in North America.

12.5M
forced into the Atlantic trade · ~10.7M disembarked alive in the Americas

ASKIf most enslaved Africans went elsewhere, why focus on the U.S.?

SRC · SlaveVoyages · PBS, “How Many Slaves Landed in the U.S.?”

1865 · ABOLITION & EXCEPTION
03/ 10

THE 13TH AMENDMENT LOOPHOLE

Emancipation arrived with an exception written into it.

The clause closes one door and leaves another open. Read it slowly — especially the comma before “except.” This is the hinge of the whole board.

Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime … shall exist within the United States.U.S. Const. amend. XIII, § 1 (1865)

ASKWhat does an exception do to a promise?

SRC · U.S. Constitution, amend. XIII · National Archives

1865–66 · RECONSTRUCTION
04/ 10

BLACK CODES & VAGRANCY

Freedom was re-criminalized. Black life became presumptively illegal.

Unemployment, loitering, migration became crimes. Any infraction — even an unpaid fine — rolled into forced labor. Same machine, new form.

…convicted of petty crimes, like walking on the grass, vagrancy, and stealing food… Even those declared innocent were often placed in this system when they could not pay their court fees.Newspaper account, Library of Congress — date to verify

ASKCan you be free if your freedom is a crime?

SRC · Studies in American Political Development (Cambridge UP) · LOC, Chronicling America (date under review)

1868–1940s · “SLAVERY BY ANOTHER NAME”
05/ 10

CONVICT LEASING

The plantation was rebuilt behind prison walls — and it turned a profit.

States leased Black convicts to plantations, mines and railroads. Brutal mortality produced profit for the state and for industry. By 1900 Black people were a large majority of Southern convicts.

~73%
estimated share of Alabama's state revenue from convict leasing, late 1800s (approx.)

ASKWhat is the difference between owning a person and renting their punishment?

SRC · Blackmon, Slavery by Another Name · Alabama Archives · LOC, Inside Adams

1890s–1960s · JIM CROW
06/ 10

THE ACCUSATION

Criminal accusation became the justification for racial terror.

The “Black rapist” myth — manufactured in media and politics — justified chain gangs, racialized policing and lynching. No trial. No evidence. Just the charge. Graphic photographs omitted by design.

~4,400
documented racial-terror lynchings of Black people, 1877–1950 (EJI)

ASKWhy did the accusation matter more than the evidence?

SRC · EJI, Lynching in America · NAACP, The Burning at Dyersburg, 15 Crisis (1917)

1971–PRESENT · WAR ON DRUGS
07/ 10

LAW & ORDER → MASS INCARCERATION

“Law and order” became the new racial control.

1971 War on Drugs. 1986 Anti-Drug Abuse Act (100:1 crack-to-cocaine). 1994 Crime Bill. Mandatory minimums. Selective enforcement.“We could arrest their leaders, raid their homes… Did we know we were lying about the drugs? Of course we did.” — John Ehrlichman (attributed/reported; disputed)

~5% / ~25%
U.S. share of world population vs. world prisoners · Black people: ~13% of the population, 37% of the incarcerated

ASKCan a law be race-neutral and still racist?

SRC · EJI · Prison Policy Initiative · ACLU-WA · World Prison Brief

2000s–PRESENT · THE ALGORITHM
08/ 10

PLANTATION → ALGORITHM

The overseer's variable judgment became the algorithm's baked-in bias.

COMPAS recidivism scoring, predictive policing, facial recognition. The bias moves from a person to a system that hides behind math — race-neutral on paper, not in outcome.

2×
Black defendants flagged high-risk at twice the rate of white defendants (ProPublica, 2016)
10–100×
higher facial-recognition false positives for Black and Asian faces (NIST, 2019)

ASKIf the algorithm cannot see race, how does it reproduce racism?

SRC · ProPublica, “Machine Bias” (2016) · NIST FRVT (2019)

BRAZIL & ARGENTINA · 1871–PRESENT
09/ 10

FREE, BUT BOUND

The U.S. is one example of a Diaspora pattern: emancipation produces new coercion.

Brazil's Law of the Free Womb (1871) “freed” newborns but bound them to their masters until 21. Argentina's liberto system conditioned freedom on military service. Afro-Argentine history was written out.

21
years Brazil's “free” children remained bound to their masters

ASKWhat does “free” mean when freedom is conditioned?

SRC · Phillips, The African Diaspora Experience · Cambridge UP · African Arguments
